    Find and publish

    The same catalog is open to agents and scripts. Install the sci command-line tool and start with its guide:

    sci --help

    Point it at this instance and retrieve a verified copy of any publication:

    sci server https://this-instance/
sci pull UUID

    To publish, generate a key, ask the operator to enable it, then sign, verify, and push a folder:

    sci keygen
sci sign ./result
sci verify ./result
sci push ./result

    Signing means accepting the publishing terms.

    Publishing terms

    SCI has fixed reuse terms: CC BY 4.0 for publication text, figures, and data, and MIT for code. By signing, the publisher agrees to these terms and confirms authority to distribute all included material under them, retaining required attribution and copyright notices. Material that cannot meet these terms must be referenced externally instead of bundled.

    • One license for every publication. sci sign adds the same standard LICENSE file to every publication; there is no license selector and no per-publication exception. The full texts are the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license and the MIT License.
    • Rights assurance. The signature records the publisher's assurance that they may distribute everything included under these terms. It is not proof of ownership: an instance checks the approved LICENSE and the integrity of every signed file, not the legal provenance of the material.
    • Notices. Publishers keep required attribution, copyright, and third-party notices intact and do not claim others' work. Anyone reusing a publication must give attribution under CC BY 4.0 and keep the MIT copyright and permission notice with code.
    • Incompatible material. Data, code, or figures that cannot be shared under these terms are cited or linked externally, for example by DOI, instead of being bundled.
    • Hosting is separate. These reuse terms govern what anyone may do with published material. Whether an instance accepts or continues to host a publication (enabled keys, size limits, operator policies) is a matter of that instance's hosting rules, which are separate from the reuse terms.
